The TIIP Model

A structured, faith-rooted framework for understanding the human soul and guiding the process of psychological healing.

Orientation

What makes TIIP distinct

Traditional Islamically Integrated Psychotherapy is not a single technique bolted onto Western therapy. It is an integrative paradigm: a coherent model of the human psyche derived from the Islamic tradition, joined to the methods and evidence base of contemporary psychology.

🕌

Tradition-rooted

Anchored in classical Islamic scholarship on the soul, the heart, and human refinement (tazkiyah).

🔬

Evidence-informed

Integrates established psychotherapeutic methods — cognitive, behavioral, emotional, and experiential.

🧭

Holistic & structured

Treats the whole person through a replicable, teachable clinical process — not ad-hoc religious advice.

Faculty of the soul

Qalb — The Spiritual Heart

The qalb is the seat of perception, faith, and moral cognition — the spiritual center where guidance and disease both take root. In TIIP, much of the therapeutic work aims to restore the heart’s clarity (ṣafāʾ al-qalb) and remove spiritual veils that distort thought and behavior.

Mechanisms of change

Four domains of intervention

TIIP locates psychological change across interacting human domains. Treatment moves fluidly among them rather than privileging any single one.

Cognition & belief

Working with thoughts, attributions, and core beliefs — including the worldview (tawḥīd-centered) lens through which a person interprets adversity, meaning, and the self. Distorted cognitions are examined both clinically and in light of sound understanding.

Emotion & the heart

Cultivating awareness, regulation, and healthy expression of emotion, while addressing “diseases of the heart” such as envy, arrogance, and despair that classical scholars identified as obstacles to well-being.

Behavior & habit

Activating adaptive behavior and refining habits (akhlāq) — using structured behavioral methods alongside the disciplining of the self toward virtuous, value-aligned action.

Spirituality & the soul

Drawing on worship, remembrance (dhikr), reflection, and connection with the Divine as genuine therapeutic resources that nourish meaning, hope, and resilience.

In the clinic

The therapeutic process

A structured arc that gives clinicians a shared, replicable roadmap — adaptable to each patient’s needs and level of faith.

Assessment & formulation

A holistic intake mapping presenting concerns across cognition, emotion, behavior, and spirituality, alongside the patient’s relationship with faith.

Alliance & meaning-making

Building trust and a shared understanding of distress that honors the patient’s worldview and frames suffering within a meaningful narrative.

Integrated intervention

Applying clinical methods together with tradition-derived practices — selected collaboratively and matched to the patient’s readiness.

Refinement of the self

Supporting growth in character and spiritual discipline (tazkiyat al-nafs) as a pathway toward lasting psychological balance.

Consolidation & relapse prevention

Embedding gains into daily life and worship, building durable habits, and preparing for future challenges.
